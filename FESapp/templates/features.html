Why it matters for doctors

Fewer calculation mistakes

  • Body surface area is worked out from the patient's weight automatically — no formulas by hand.
  • Every adjustment to a dose (body size, upper limit, organ safety) is calculated and shown, not done on paper.
  • The same patient details always give the same plan, so every result can be checked and reproduced.

See the impact before the first dose

  • How the tumour is expected to shrink over the whole course, from the first dose to day 120.
  • The side-effect load after every dose, and how it recovers in between.
  • Change the weight, the interval or the organ tolerance and see the new plan in under a second.
{% if example %}See the projected response →{% endif %}

Every dose checked against 13 organs

  • Predicts how much drug builds up in the lungs, heart, kidneys, liver, brain and 8 more over the 48 hours after each dose.
  • Lowers a dose before it is given if any organ would go past its safe limit.
  • Names the organ that limited each dose — for example, “lung: would reach 15.0, limit 13.0”.
{% if example %}See the organ checks →{% endif %}

Thinks the way a doctor does

  • Its 51 rules try to mimic how an oncologist thinks: weigh how big the tumour is against the side effects so far, then adjust for the patient's body size.
  • Written in a doctor's own terms — “tumour very big and side effects low → give a very high dose”.
  • Each dose comes with a plain explanation, and can be traced back step by step to its cause — no black box.

Personalised to each patient

  • Adjusts for body size, and for how much tumour there is — from molecular remission to very bulky disease.
  • Tolerance profiles for patients with reduced kidney or heart function.
  • Doses ease off as the tumour responds, instead of repeating one fixed amount.

Under the hood

Two fuzzy expert systems (FES-1 and FES-2), with 51 rules that try to mimic how an oncologist thinks, set each dose from tumour size, side-effect load and body size. A physiologically based pharmacokinetic (PBPK) model with 35 compartments predicts how the drug spreads through 13 organs, and a tumour growth model projects the response over the course.

Research status

A research prototype built on a peer-reviewed model. The organ safe limits are illustrative, and the model has not yet been calibrated against patient data, so any clinical use would first need validation.
